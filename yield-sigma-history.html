Notes

Reading the line. Above +1σ the dividend yield is high against the stock's own history, so the stock price is conversely low (undervalued). Below −1σ the dividend yield is low and the stock price conversely high (overvalued).

Bars. Each bar is what actually happened: the total return (stock price change plus dividends) over the 24 months after buying in that month, or 12 months with the switch above the bars. Green is a gain, rust a loss; the darker the shade, the bigger the move. The most recent months have no bar yet.

Months at each level. The panel on the right counts how many months in the period shown the reading spent at each level, in steps of 0.25σ, on the same scale as the line. The outlined, lightly shaded blocks behind them add the months up in wider steps (1σ or ½σ, picked in the menu under the panel), with the share of all months in the period shown in bold at the right. The outlined level is this month; hovering the line highlights the level of the month under the cursor. Hover a bar in the panel to shade that level across the line chart and mark every month that sat in it. Flip to bell turns the panel on its side into a bell curve.

Hovering. Hover any month on the bars or the line: the line above the bars shows the month bought, the actual total return over the next 24 (or 12) months, the σ predicted return and the total return to today. The reading appears in large type next to the line, with the dividend yield and stock price along the top of the chart, and a % dot marks the month on the dividend chart, larger when that month's dividend yield sat higher in the stock's own range.

Dividend amount. The panel under the line shows the dividend amount per year in dollars, with its growth over the prior year written inside each block. Hover it for the yearly increase or decrease and that year's average dividend yield.

Stretches outside the normal range. The area between the line and ±1σ is shaded wherever the reading is outside the normal range, green above +1σ and rust below −1σ. A dip back inside for 3 months or less, then out again on the same side, counts as the same stretch. The table lists stretches of 3 months or more, plus any still running: how long each lasted, its most extreme reading, and what buying in its first month paid over the next 12 and 24 months.

σ predicted vs actual. The table groups the months by Yield Sigma™ at purchase and compares the average σ predicted return with the average actual return. The Labs note beside its title explains how σ predicted is worked out.

Projection fan. The fan after today is a projection, not history. It comes from a mean-reversion regression on the stock's own monthly readings (next month's reading against this month's, pulling toward 0). The dashed line is the most likely path; the darker band holds half of the likely outcomes, the lighter band 9 in 10.

Point-in-time. Every reading is point-in-time: each month's dividend yield uses only the dividends paid in the 12 months up to that month, measured against the prior 10 years of dividend yields.

Data. Monthly closing prices from Yahoo Finance (identical to STOCKHISTORY from 2016 on). Dividend amount per year: regular dividends paid, from EODHD, or cleaned Yahoo Finance data for stocks not yet switched over; special dividends (e.g. TROW 2012, 2015, 2021) are left out of both yields and total returns. Yearly yield (for the 10-year history) = regular dividends that year ÷ average monthly closing price that year. Monthly dividend yield = regular dividends paid in the past 12 months (each year's total spread evenly across its months) ÷ that month's closing price; 2026 uses the Div$ column. Bars and tables are historical results; σ predicted and the projection fan are statistical estimates, not guarantees.
